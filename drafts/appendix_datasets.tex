\section{Datasets, Causal Roles, and Actionability}
\label{app:data}

This appendix describes each dataset, its preprocessing, and the role of
every variable under the SFM projection (Definition~\ref{def:sfm}). For each
dataset, $x^{(0)}$ denotes the disadvantaged group. In every dataset, the
mediated paths through $W$ are treated as the disparity that recourse should
address, and the direct path $X\to\hat Y$ is reported but cannot be reached
by recourse. Sample sizes refer to one train/validation split
(Table~\ref{tab:datasets}).

\subsection{Data Sources and Preprocessing}
\label{app:data_sources}

\paragraph{German-Synth.}
We adapt the semi-synthetic German Credit SCM
of~\citet{karimi2020imperfect} to the block structure of
Section~\ref{sec:flows}. Sex $X$ (\texttt{SEX}) $\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)$
(male $=x^{(1)}$) and age $Z$ (\texttt{AGE}; $19+\mathrm{Gamma}(3,6)$, clipped
to $[19,75]$) are independent roots. Education (\texttt{EDUCATION}), job skill
level (\texttt{JOB}), and savings (\texttt{SAVINGS\_GRP}) are four-level
ordinal mediators, each generated by an ordered-logit mechanism from sex, age,
and the preceding mediator. Credit amount (\texttt{CREDIT\_AMOUNT}) and
duration (\texttt{DURATION}) are log-normal, depend on age, job, and savings
but not directly on sex, share exogenous noise with correlation $0.8$, and are
clipped to the UCI ranges (250--18{,}424\,DM and 4--72 months). The outcome
(\texttt{GOOD\_CREDIT}) is Bernoulli with a logistic link in sex, age, all
mediators, and a savings--amount interaction. All sex effects on mediators
favor men. Two changes from Karimi et al.\ keep our assumptions exactly
true. Their amount$\to$duration edge would lie inside one unordered block, so
we replace it with correlated noise. Their continuous income is replaced by
the ordinal job level. Because the mediator law is known, oracle
classifier-scale effects are computed by sampling the true mechanisms.

\paragraph{Structural equations.}
Let $a=(Z-35)/11$, and let $\mathrm{OL}(m;c_1,c_2,c_3)$ draw a latent
$m+\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\sim\mathrm{Logistic}(0,1)$ and return the number
of cutpoints below it. With mediation scale $\kappa\ge0$,
\begin{align*}
  E &= \mathrm{OL}(1.2\kappa X+0.3a;\;-1.0,\,0.6,\,2.2),\\
  J &= \mathrm{OL}(1.0\kappa X+0.3a+1.0E;\;-0.5,\,1.4,\,3.4),\\
  S &= \mathrm{OL}(0.9\kappa X+0.5a+0.7J;\;0.3,\,1.8,\,3.0),\\
  \log L &= 7.7+0.1a+0.1J-0.1S+0.7u_1,\qquad
  \log D = 3.35-0.05a-0.15J-0.12S+0.4u_2,
\end{align*}
with $(u_1,u_2)$ standard bivariate normal with correlation $0.8$, $L$
clipped to $[250,18{,}424]$ and $D$ to $[4,72]$. With
$\ell=(\log L-7.75)/0.75$ and $d=(\log D-2.9)/0.45$,
\begin{equation*}
  \operatorname{logit}P(Y=1\mid\cdot)=0.85+0.3X+0.3a+0.2(E-1.5)+0.6(J-1.5)
  +0.6(S-1.5)-0.7\ell-0.8d+0.25(S-1.5)\ell .
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Mediation stress test.}
The scale $\kappa$ multiplies every $X\to$ mediator coefficient. It
therefore changes how far sex shifts the mediators, and with it the $\NIE$,
while the female mediator law and the outcome equation, and hence the $\NDE$,
stay fixed. We use four versions (Table~\ref{tab:synth_versions}).
$\kappa=1$ is the benchmark in the main results. $\kappa=0$ is a null case
with no mediated disparity. Each version draws one pool of 10{,}000 units
with generator seed 0; each experimental seed splits it into 8{,}000
training and 2{,}000 validation units.

\paragraph{Law School.}
We use the 1991 LSAC National Longitudinal Bar Passage
Study~\citep{wightman1998lsac}, in the version distributed with the R package
\texttt{fairml}~0.9.1 (\texttt{law.school.admissions}). We keep Black
($x^{(0)}$) and White ($x^{(1)}$) respondents (\texttt{race1}, recoded as the
binary \texttt{race}) with non-missing gender and family-income bracket, which
leaves 18{,}694 respondents, 6.4\% of them Black. The pre-treatment covariates
are gender (\texttt{gender}, male $=1$) and family-income bracket
(\texttt{fam\_inc}, five ordered levels coded 1--5). The mediators are the LSAT
score (\texttt{lsat}, rounded to the nearest integer, range 11--48) and
undergraduate GPA (\texttt{gpa}, renamed from \texttt{ugpa}, range 0--4).
Both enter the classifiers as standardized numeric features, and family
income enters as its integer code. The outcome is first-attempt bar passage
(\texttt{bar\_pass}, from \texttt{bar}; positive rate 0.90). All other columns
of the source table are dropped. Each seed draws an 80/20 split.

\paragraph{ACS Income.}
We use the 2019 one-year ACS PUMS person file for California and New York,
obtained with \texttt{folktables}~\citep{ding2021retiring} and filtered as in
its income task (age $>16$, income $>\$100$, positive hours and weeks
worked, person weight $\ge1$). Rows missing any analysed field are dropped,
which leaves 301{,}523 respondents. The outcome (\texttt{income}) indicates
personal income \texttt{PINCP} above \$50{,}000 (positive rate 0.44), and sex
(\texttt{SEX}) is the sensitive attribute (female $=x^{(0)}$). The
pre-treatment covariates are age (\texttt{AGEP}, in years, standardized) and
place of birth (\texttt{POBP}), binarized as \texttt{POBP\_US} (born in a US
state or territory, codes 1--56, versus abroad). Education (\texttt{SCHL}, 24
levels) is grouped as \texttt{SCHL\_GRP} into six levels: no high-school
diploma (1--15), high school (16--17), some college or associate degree
(18--20), bachelor's (21), master's or professional (22--23), and doctorate
(24). Occupation (\texttt{OCCP}, about 500 census codes) is grouped as
\texttt{OCCP\_GRP} into ten groups of contiguous 2018 census codes:
management (10--430); business operations (500--740); financial specialists
(800--960); computer and mathematical (1000--1240); architecture and
engineering (1300--1560); life, physical, and social science (1600--1980);
community and social service, legal, education, and arts and media
(2000--2920); healthcare, protective service, and food preparation
(3000--4160); cleaning, personal care, sales, and office support
(4200--5940); and farming, construction, maintenance, production, and
transportation (6000--9750). The 3.5\% of respondents whose code lies outside
these ranges, mostly ``other managers'' (440) and the military (9800--9830),
are assigned to the community/legal/education/arts group. Usual weekly hours
(\texttt{WKHP}) and weeks worked in the past year (\texttt{WKWN}) are kept
in natural units and clipped to $[1,60]$ and $[1,52]$. In the classifiers the
two categorical mediators are one-hot encoded and the continuous features are
standardized. Class of worker (\texttt{COW}), marital status
(\texttt{MAR}), relationship (\texttt{RELP}), and race (\texttt{RAC1P}) are
not used. Each seed draws an 80/20 split.

\paragraph{Adult.}
We combine the canonical UCI Adult training and test files~\citep{becker1996adult}
and drop rows with missing values in any analysed field, which leaves
45{,}222 rows. The outcome (\texttt{income}) indicates income above \$50{,}000
(positive rate 0.25), and sex (\texttt{SEX}, from \texttt{sex}) is the
sensitive attribute (female $=x^{(0)}$). The pre-treatment covariates are age
(\texttt{AGE}, from \texttt{age}, in years, standardized) and
\texttt{NATIVE\_US}, which indicates \texttt{native-country} equal to the
United States. Education (\texttt{education-num}, 16 levels) is grouped as
\texttt{EDUCATION\_GRP} into six ordered levels that mirror ACS: below high
school (1--8), high-school graduate (9), some college or associate degree
(10--12), bachelor's (13), master's (14), and professional school or doctorate
(15--16). Occupation (\texttt{occupation}) keeps its 14 UCI categories as
\texttt{OCCUPATION\_GRP}. Weekly hours (\texttt{hours-per-week}) are clipped
to $[1,60]$ and binned as \texttt{HOURS\_GRP} into eight ordered categories
(1--19, 20--29, 30--34, 35--39, 40, 41--49, 50--59, 60), which preserves the
point mass at 40 hours. All mediators are therefore categorical, and the
mediator model has no flow component. All three mediators are one-hot encoded
in the classifiers. Work class, marital status, relationship, race, capital
gain and loss, \texttt{fnlwgt}, and the string-valued \texttt{education} (a
duplicate of \texttt{education-num}) are not used. Each seed draws an 80/20
split.

\subsection{Role Assignment and Actionability}
\label{app:roles}

Table~\ref{tab:roles} lists the role of each variable and the actionability
type, grid step, and bounds of each mediator. A variable is placed in $Z$
only if it is fixed before $X$ could affect it, and in $W$ if the literature
identifies it as a consequence of $X$ that precedes the decision
(Section~\ref{sec:projection}). Discrete monotone mediators may move to any
higher category, and free mediators to any category. Continuous mediators
move on a grid with the stated step between the factual value and the bound
in the permitted direction. The factual value is always in the grid.
